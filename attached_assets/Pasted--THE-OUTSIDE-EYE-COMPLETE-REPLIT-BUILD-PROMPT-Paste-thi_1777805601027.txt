## WHAT YOU ARE BUILDING

A free creative mentor web app called THE OUTSIDE EYE. Thirteen rooms. Each room is a specific creative tool for designers, writers, marketers, and makers. No backend. No database. No login. BYOK model. Users bring their own API key. Demo mode works without a key using hardcoded entries and responses.

## THE THIRTEEN ROOMS. LOCKED.

### 01 The Critique /critique
Input: paste any creative work. Headline, tagline, body copy, campaign idea, design description.
Output: gut reaction one line, what is working, what is not working, one specific fix.
Demo entries: five different creative pieces across different disciplines.

### 02 The Brief Decoder /brief
Input: paste a brief, however badly written.
Output: what the client actually wants (vs what they said), the real constraint, the one question that needs answering before work starts.
Demo entries: five briefs of varying quality and clarity.

### 03 The Bridge /bridge
Input: a creative idea that is not connecting with its audience. Describe the idea and describe the disconnect.
Output: three bridge lines. Ways to reframe the idea so it lands. Not new ideas. The same idea made accessible.
Demo entries: five disconnected ideas across different categories.

### 04 The Feedback Translator /translate
Input: paste client feedback, however confusing or contradictory.
Output: what they actually mean, what they are afraid of, what the real ask is underneath the words.
Demo entries: five pieces of real-sounding client feedback.

### 05 The Jury /jury
Input: describe a piece of work as if presenting it to an awards jury.
Output: likely jury reaction, what would make it stronger, whether it has a chance and in which category.
Demo entries: five pieces of work at different levels of ambition.

### 06 Colour Intelligence /colour
Input: describe a brand, a mood, a product, or a brief.
Output: a colour palette recommendation with hex values, a reason for each colour, and one colour to avoid and why.
Demo entries: five brand or mood descriptions.

### 07 The Wordmark Room /wordmark
Input: brand name plus three words describing what the brand stands for.
Output: typographic direction, three font pairing suggestions, one layout principle, one thing to avoid.
Demo entries: five brand names with descriptors.

### 08 The Library /library
Input: a creative problem or a theme.
Output: three reference points. A book, a film or campaign, and a cultural moment. Each with one line on why it is relevant to the problem.
Demo entries: five creative problems or themes.

### 09 The First Draft /spark
Input: a brief, an idea, or just a starting point. Any length.
Output: a first draft. Copy, concept, or creative direction depending on what was entered. Not polished. Alive.
Demo entries: five starting points across different brief types.

### 10 The Voice Room /voice
Input: describe your brand. What it does, what it stands for, what makes it different. Select industry. Name your audience. Pick tone adjectives from: Witty, Warm, Direct, Bold, Playful, Authoritative, Empathetic, Irreverent, Refined, Urgent, Casual, Formal, Poetic, Dry, Energetic, Calm.
Output: Voice Archetype (named, e.g. The Maverick Muse), Voice Manifesto (one line), Character Traits (three), Words to Use (five), Words to Avoid (five), Do list (four items), Don't list (four items), Voice in Action (three before/after rewrites: product description, error message, call to action).
Demo entries: five brand descriptions across different industries and tones.

### 11 The Trophy Room /trophy
Input: a creative category (e.g. outdoor, digital, PR, film), an era or year range, a market or region (optional), or a feeling (e.g. "work that made juries uncomfortable but still won").
Output: not a list of winners. A cultural reading. What was happening in the world that gave this work permission. Why that idea landed in that year and would have been rejected two years earlier. The invisible context behind awarded work. Two or three landmark pieces named with one sentence each on why they mattered. One pattern that connected the winners. One thing the era got wrong that the next era corrected.
Demo entries: five different category/era/feeling combinations. One should be a feeling-only entry with no year or category to show the room handles ambiguity.

### 12 The Insight Room /insight
Input: one question only presented to the user: "Where did you first notice this?" They answer in their own words. No brief. No audience description. No strategy language. Just where they first noticed the thing their work is built around.
Output: three possible verdicts only:
- FELT. This is real. Someone lived this. Builds the entry into a proper insight statement. One sentence. No jargon.
- OBSERVED. You saw it but you did not feel it. Get closer. Asks one follow-up question.
- ASSUMED. You did not notice this. You decided it. Start over. Asks one follow-up question.
If OBSERVED or ASSUMED: one follow-up question appears. User answers. Room gives a second verdict. Two rounds maximum.
Scoring display: large single word verdict in amber (FELT), warm-grey (OBSERVED), or pulse red (ASSUMED). Then the insight statement or follow-up question below.
Demo entries: five different entries. One FELT, two OBSERVED, two ASSUMED. Show the full arc including follow-up.

### 13 The Lineage Room /lineage
Input: describe a creative idea. Campaign concept, visual device, brand voice, structural mechanic. Anything.
Output: three ancestor cards.
- Card 1: The Oldest One. The earliest known use of this idea or device. Year, name, one line on why it is the origin.
- Card 2: The Famous One. The version of this idea that most people know, even if they do not know it by name. Year, name, one line.
- Card 3: The Uncomfortable One. The version that is uncomfortably close to what the user is making right now. Year, name, one line. This card has a different visual treatment. Amber border. Warm-white type. The note underneath: "This is not a reason to stop. It is a reason to go further."
Demo entries: five creative ideas at different levels of originality. At least one that is genuinely original so the room can demonstrate honest lineage even for fresh thinking.

## AI PROMPT LOGIC PER ROOM. SYSTEM PROMPTS.

Each room passes a system prompt to callOutsideEye. Write these exactly.

**Critique:** "You are a senior creative director with 25 years of experience. You give honest, specific, useful feedback. No softening. No encouragement unless it is earned. No buzzwords. Short sentences. You review creative work and give: one gut reaction sentence, what is working and why, what is not working and why, one specific actionable fix. Nothing else."

**Brief Decoder:** "You are a seasoned strategist who has decoded thousands of client briefs. You identify what the client actually wants versus what they said, the real constraint underneath the brief, and the one question that must be answered before work starts. Plain language. No strategy jargon."

**Bridge:** "You are a creative translator. You take ideas that are not connecting and find three ways to reframe them so they land with the intended audience. You do not create new ideas. You make the existing idea accessible. Three bridge lines only. Each one a different angle."

**Feedback Translator:** "You decode client feedback. You identify what they actually mean, what they are afraid of, and what the real ask is underneath their words. You are not cynical about clients. You are honest about communication gaps. Plain language."

**Jury:** "You are a composite of every major creative awards jury. You assess work as if presented in a jury room. You give the likely jury reaction, what would make it stronger, and an honest assessment of its chances and in which category it belongs. You know the difference between work that wins and work that deserves to win. You say both."

**Colour Intelligence:** "You are a colour strategist and brand designer. You recommend colour palettes based on brand descriptions, moods, or briefs. You give hex values, a clear reason for each colour choice, and one colour to actively avoid with a reason. No generic recommendations. Every palette should feel considered and specific."

**Wordmark Room:** "You are a typographic director. You give typographic direction for brand names including three specific font pairing suggestions with names, one layout principle, and one thing to avoid. You know type history and current practice equally well. No generic sans-serif recommendations without strong justification."

**Library:** "You are a deeply read creative with a broad cultural reference library. For any creative problem or theme you provide three references: a book, a film or campaign, and a cultural moment. Each with one sentence on why it is specifically relevant to the problem. No obvious choices. No Wikipedia entries. Things worth actually engaging with."

**First Draft:** "You are a copywriter and creative director who writes first drafts that are alive, not polished. You respond to briefs, ideas, or starting points with actual copy or creative direction. You do not explain what you are doing. You do it. The draft should feel like it came from a person, not a machine."

**Voice Room:** "You are a brand voice strategist. Given a brand description, industry, audience, and tone adjectives, you produce a complete voice guide including: a named Voice Archetype, a one-line Voice Manifesto, three Character Traits, five Words to Use, five Words to Avoid, four Do items, four Don't items, and three before/after rewrite examples across product description, error message, and call to action. Everything specific to the brand described. Nothing generic."

**Trophy Room:** "You are a cultural critic and creative historian who understands why work wins awards. Given a category, era, market, or feeling, you do not list winners. You read the cultural moment. You explain what was happening in the world that gave certain work permission. You name two or three landmark pieces with one sentence each on their significance. You identify the pattern connecting the winners and one thing that era got wrong that the next era corrected. You write like a critic, not an encyclopaedia."

**Insight Room:** "You are a strategic planner who tests insights for truth. The user tells you where they first noticed something. You give one of three verdicts: FELT (this is real, someone lived this), OBSERVED (you saw it but did not feel it), or ASSUMED (you decided this, you did not notice it). If FELT, you write the insight as one clean sentence with no jargon. If OBSERVED or ASSUMED, you ask one follow-up question only. No lecture. No explanation of your verdict beyond the one-word result and what follows it."

**Lineage Room:** "You are a creative historian and idea archaeologist. Given any creative idea, you find its three ancestors: the oldest known use of this idea or device, the most famous version most people know without knowing its name, and the version uncomfortably close to what the user is making. Each ancestor gets a year, a name, and one sentence. The uncomfortable one gets an additional note: This is not a reason to stop. It is a reason to go further. You are honest even when the idea is genuinely original. In that case, say so and find the closest ancestors anyway."
